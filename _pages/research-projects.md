Here are the details of my most important research projects and internships.

---
* **2025** - **The Long Term Impact of Parental Job Loss on Children's Education in Germany and the UK.** (*Master's Thesis*). My thesis examines the intergenerational spillovers of involuntary parental job loss on children's educational trajectories in the UK and Germany, using data from BHPS and Understanding Society (1991–2024) and the German Socio-Economic Panel (1984–2024). Through a quasi-experimental design (across three identification strategies) I estimate the causal effect of parental job loss on children's educational attainment across two institutionally distinct welfare regimes, and use KHB decomposition to disentangle and identify the micro-level pathways (income, health, stress) through which this disruption emerge. The results show negative effects that are robust across all designs in both countries, but reveal that maternal and paternal job loss are not homogeneous treatments: they operate through different mechanisms depending on labour market and educational institutions, working mainly through acute income shocks in the UK and through early tracking exposure and strong path dependency in Germany.
  
* **2025** - **Parental Divorce and Children's Educational Trajectories**. This empirical research focuses on understanding the consequences of parental divorce on children's educational pathways across countries and birth cohorts. The project employs GGS and FReDA data and uses a multilevel design for the analysis.

* **2024** - **Prospective Educational Mobility in Italy**. This empirical research focuses on educational and social mobility using longitudinal data from the Bank of Italy, SHIW (Survey on Income and Wealth) and a prospective approach.
